A quantum particle is a wave. Fire one at a wall it cannot climb and part of it gets through anyway. Pick a landscape, set the packet's energy, and compare what the simulation measures with the exact answer.

Keys: Space plays and pauses, R resets, [ and ] lower and raise the energy, P toggles painting. With painting on, drag across the plot to draw a landscape. Height shows |ψ|², colour shows the phase of ψ.

Transmission spectrum

The blue curve is the exact transmission of a plane wave at each kinetic energy; the orange shading is how the packet's probability is spread over those energies, and the dashed line marks its mean. Each finished run adds a dot (measured) inside a ring (exact for that packet). Click the spectrum to fire a packet at that energy.

Bound states

A confining well has no transmission spectrum: the packet can never leave. Instead it is a fixed mix of the well's stationary states, drawn as thin lines on the plot above. Each bar is the share of the packet in one state, at that state's energy; the shares do not change as the packet moves.

View

Units with ħ = m = 1, so a packet with wavenumber k has energy k²/2 and moves at speed k. The wave is advanced with the split-step Fourier method on 2048 points; absorbing layers beyond the visible window soak up what leaves, and their intake is counted as reflected or transmitted. The exact columns come from transfer matrices across the same grid cells. Source on GitHub.
